# Right-Sizing Your Team

**A workplace training module on staffing ratios for new contact center team leads.**

Instructional design portfolio project by [Unsi Rathod](https://github.com/unsi-rathod). Status: design complete, not yet built in an authoring tool.

## What this is

New team leads often get promoted for being good at the job, then have to plan rosters with little training. This project is a 15-minute self-paced eLearning course that teaches one repeatable way to answer "how many people do I need on this shift?"

The maths is proportional reasoning. I took my own Class VIII lesson design on the topic and rebuilt it for adults at work. The idli batter, spice mix and road work examples became a Monday shift that is three reps short, a promo email that pushes up the forecast, two people calling in sick and an email backlog.

## Learning objectives

After the course, learners can:

1. Read a staffing ratio and say what it means
2. Work out how many reps a shift needs, and split reps across queues
3. Adjust when the forecast changes or people are off
4. Choose between staffing options and justify the choice with numbers

## From school lesson to workplace

| In the school lesson | In this course |
|---|---|
| Equivalent ratios, cross-multiplication | Reps needed for a call forecast, and checking a ratio |
| Scaling a multi-term ratio by one factor | Forecast goes up, team goes up by the same factor |
| Inverse proportion (workers and days) | Absences and the email backlog |
| Dividing a whole in a ratio | Splitting the team across Voice, Chat and Email |
| Working together at different speeds | Trainees take fewer calls than experienced reps |

## Design decisions

- **Problem first, method second.** The Monday scene comes before any formula. Learners record a guess that the course returns to after the worked example.
- **Feedback that names the mistake.** Wrong answers get a specific explanation, for example "12 is the gap in calls an hour, not in reps", rather than "try again".
- **A decision with no single right answer.** In the Friday scenario each option fixes one problem and creates another. The strongest answer combines two options.
