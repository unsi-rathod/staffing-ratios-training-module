# Design Brief: Right-Sizing Your Team

Staffing ratios for new team leads. Self-paced eLearning, about 15 minutes.

## Business problem

New team leads are usually promoted for strong performance, not for planning skills. When they misjudge how many reps a shift needs, queues grow and people burn out, or the team is overstaffed and costs rise. This course gives them a simple, repeatable way to reason about staffing ratios.

## Learner profile

- New team leads, 0 to 6 months in the role, with strong operational knowledge
- Uneven confidence with maths
- Short on time between shifts, working on a desktop in short blocks, so the course runs about 15 minutes and can be paused

## Learning objectives

After the course, learners can:

1. **O1.** Explain what a staffing ratio is and read one from a roster
2. **O2.** Work out how many reps a shift needs, and split reps across queues
3. **O3.** Adjust the numbers when the forecast changes or people are off, using proportional reasoning
4. **O4.** Choose between staffing options in a scenario and justify the choice with numbers

## Design approach

- **Scenario first.** Learners act as a team lead facing a real staffing decision before any formula appears.
- **Build in steps.** Ratio, then ratio table, then scaling, then inverse relationships, then a decision.
- **Adapted from an existing lesson.** The structure and maths come from my Class VIII Proportional Reasoning-2 lesson design (see `source/`): a familiar hook, a worked example, then guided practice.

## Assessment and evaluation

- Scenario-based practice with feedback that explains why an answer is right or wrong (screens 6, 8, 9, 10)
- A final check drawn from the question bank (`03-question-bank.pdf`)
- Kirkpatrick level 1: reaction survey on the last screen
- Kirkpatrick level 2: scored items during the course
- Kirkpatrick level 3: a short follow-up 30 days later asking whether the cheat sheet has been used

## Assumptions

- The company (BrightLine Support), the characters and all figures are made up. One rep handles about 6 calls an hour; a trainee handles 4.
- Real rosters also have to allow for breaks, training and shrinkage. The course says this on screen 4.
- The tone is friendly and practical.
